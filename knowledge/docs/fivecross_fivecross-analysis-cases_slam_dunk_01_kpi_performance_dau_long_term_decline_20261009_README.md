H1: raw whole-28-day absence among baseline frequent, recently nonpaying non-HOF accounts was 3.46% (May), 3.41% (June), and 5.25% (July). The immediate June-release mass-exit claim is not established. Among comparable frequent players, including recent payers, activity/payment/rank-adjusted July absence was 4.04% for non-HOF vs 2.21% for HOF; higher non-HOF absence also appears in August/September. These associations cannot establish subjective dissatisfaction or causality.

H2: among recent payers still active at least seven days in the first follow-up fortnight, stopping PURCHASE is associated with weaker subsequent return in some basic cuts. Once exact first-fortnight activity is also aligned, adjusted differences across five anchors range from −0.51 to +0.29 percentage points, with no clear differences. The stricter result only covers the common-support still-active subset, and does not prove all payment channels or all departing payers behaved this way.

H3: within observed completed-match waiting quartiles, and controlling dominant rank, baseline activity, payment, match frequency, HOF holding and dominant event-hour bucket, August adjusted whole-28-day absence is 13.11% for the longest-wait quartile versus 9.53% for the shortest (eligible N 1,416 vs 1,902). The +3.58-point difference has an exploratory approximate 95% interval of +1.48 to +5.69 points. September has no clear difference after clock controls. Matching friction is a testable intervention lead, not a proven universal cause; abandoned queues and timestamp timezone are not independently verified. All uncertainty is exploratory, without multiplicity correction; boundary variance uses half-count stabilization to avoid zero-width intervals.

Queries 12–16 inspect event coverage, prospective 14-day continuation by prior payment/activity, completed ranked-match waiting, and pre-window Hall-of-Fame ownership. `scripts/analyze_churn_exploration.py` retains reconciled diagnostic outputs in `outputs/churn_exploration_20261013/`. `scripts/fetch_tw_appstore_reviews.py` retrieves the latest 500 public Taiwan App Store RSS reviews, preserving updated dates converted to Taiwan time in selected evidence.

The current evidence supports prioritizing character progression/competitive fairness, purchaser trust, and matchmaking friction as distinct hypotheses. It does **not** establish a single causal explanation. Among older/registration-unobserved accounts active at least 7 of the preceding 14 days and with no valid PURCHASE in those 14 days, observed non-HOF holders' next-14-day continuation was 95.75% for the June 4 anchor and 93.44% for the June 18 anchor; observed HOF holders were 98.37% and 98.52%. These are different prospective cohorts, not a causal before/after estimate. Ownership includes confirmed Sakuragi/Rukawa/Miyagi IDs and excludes missing owned-card snapshots from holder comparisons.

Public feedback includes a June 5 (Taiwan updated date) self-reported deletion after seeing HOF Sakuragi/Rukawa, and an August 12 paying Rukawa user's complaint about bans and subsequent weakening. These are individual accounts of dissatisfaction, not representative incidence or verification of official balance changes. Matchmaking complaints predate June. In completed matches in the 120–149 pre-match star group, the September raw average waiting field rose 49.7% versus August, but this is not a game-wide increase and excludes cancelled/unmatched queues; the field's physical unit remains unverified. April combat events cover only 18 days, and May star extraction is incomplete, so neither is used as a full-month comparable match-wait baseline.

Recall interventions should be tested separately for non-HOF players (usable character access/fair competition), disappointed purchasers (balance/bans and investment reassurance), and returning players (match availability and progression catch-up), with a control group and sustained activity outcomes. No recall messages were sent and no operational change was made.
